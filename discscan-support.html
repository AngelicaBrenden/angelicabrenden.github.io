How does Discscan know how a disc flies?

Every disc has four flight numbers on the stamp — speed, glide, turn and fade. They describe the flight for a player who throws just hard enough for that disc. Discscan uses them together with how far you throw, so the curve shows how the disc flies for you. A fast driver in a newer player's hand often fades early and lands short; the app shows that instead of the pro curve.

The curve is a guide, not a promise. Wind, the angle you release at and the plastic all change a real throw. Log a few of your own throws on a disc, and the app adjusts its curve to match them.

The scan didn't find my disc

  • Fill the frame with the stamp, and avoid glare from the sun or a lamp.
  • Shiny, foil or very artistic stamps are harder to read. If the right disc is in the list, just tap it.
  • Some newer discs are not in the app yet. Tap Add it yourself and type the four numbers from the stamp — it then works like any other disc.

Setting how far you throw

Everything in the app depends on this number. Set it under your throw settings, or tap Measure to measure a throw with GPS: mark where you throw from, walk to the disc and mark it. GPS on a phone is accurate to a few metres, so measure two or three throws if you can.

Choosing a disc from a tee sign

In Which Disc?, tap Use a Tee Sign and take a photo of the hole map. The app reads the distance from the sign; then trace the fairway with your finger from the tee to the basket. It suggests a disc from your bag, the angle to throw it at and how hard.

Location and camera

The camera is used to read the stamp on a disc and the map on a tee sign. Your location is used only while you measure a throw. If you said no and want to change your mind, it is in the iPhone's own Settings, under Discscan.
